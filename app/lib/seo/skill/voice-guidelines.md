## Voice profiles

Openclaw articles default to **Conversational Expert**: knowledgeable but accessible,
direct but warm, confident without being arrogant.

| Profile | Use for | Character |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Conversational Expert | Blog posts, guides, how-tos | Smart friend who knows a lot about the topic |
| Professional | B2B, high-ticket, technical | Authoritative, data-driven, formal but not stiff |
| Friendly | Lifestyle, gifting, beginner content | Warm, encouraging, no jargon |
| Expert | Medical, scientific, technical | Precise, cited, cautious with claims |

---

## Conversational Expert — core patterns

**Do:**
- Use second person ("you", "your") throughout
- Use contractions ("you'll", "it's", "that's")
- State the answer before explaining it
- Use specific numbers and facts ("studies show 73% of runners..." not "many runners...")
- Break complex ideas into short sentences after stating them
- Use "because" to explain reasoning, not just assert it

**Don't:**
- Use passive voice when active is available
- Start with "In this article" or "Today we're going to"
- Use corporate filler: "leverage", "utilize", "facilitate", "synergy"
- Use hedge words without reason: "might", "could", "possibly", "perhaps"
- Over-qualify: "it's important to note that" → just note it
- Write in third person about the reader ("shoppers should consider...")

## Tone by article type

### Best-of / Roundup (COMMERCIAL intent)
Tone: confident, opinionated, decisive.
- Give clear winners: "The best overall pick is X, and here's why."
- Don't hedge: "We think X might be a good option" → "X is the best option for Y people because Z."
- Use comparison table early, before long explanations.

### How-to / Tutorial (INFORMATIONAL)
Tone: patient, encouraging, clear.
- Number steps and keep them atomic (one action per step).
- Anticipate confusion: "If you see X, that's normal — it means Y."
- End steps with what the user should see/have: "After this step, your pan should be evenly coated."

### Comparison / Versus (COMMERCIAL)
Tone: balanced but ultimately decisive.
- Acknowledge both sides genuinely, then give a recommendation.
- "X is better for Y people. Z is better for W people. If you're [description], go with X."
- Never leave the reader without a recommendation.

### Educational / Pillar (INFORMATIONAL)
Tone: thorough, referenced, trustworthy.
- Cite specific sources, studies, expert names.
- Use "according to [source]" not "experts say".
- Be willing to say "we don't know yet" when research is inconclusive.

## Sentence structure patterns

**Lead with the point:**
- Bad: "There are many factors to consider when choosing running shoes, and one of them is arch support."
- Good: "Arch support is the most important factor when choosing running shoes for flat feet."

**Use parallel structure in lists:**
- Bad: "You should consider the cushioning, also the arch support matters, and don't forget about durability."
- Good: "Consider cushioning, arch support, and durability."

**Short sentences after complex ones:**
- "Overpronation occurs when your foot rolls inward more than 15% during a stride, placing stress on the ankle, knee, and hip joints. Most people overpronate to some degree. Only about 20% have severe overpronation requiring motion control shoes."

**Transition words that work:**
- "That said," (contrast)
- "Here's the thing:" (introducing key insight)
- "The short answer is" (BLUF pattern)
- "In practice," (real-world application)
- "Specifically," (narrowing)

## What to avoid — concrete examples

| Bad | Good |
|-----|------|
| "It is important to note that..." | Just say the thing. |
| "In order to achieve optimal results..." | "To get the best results..." |
| "There are a number of ways..." | "There are three ways..." |
| "At the end of the day..." | Cut it. |
| "To summarize what we've covered..." | Skip to the CTA. |
| "Feel free to reach out if you have questions." | Specific CTA only. |
| "Running shoes are an important part of any runner's kit." | Start with the answer. |
| "As we mentioned earlier in this article..." | Don't self-reference. |

## Opening patterns (after BLUF)

Never start the BLUF with:
- "Are you looking for..."
- "Have you ever wondered..."
- "In this article..."
- "Welcome to our guide on..."

Good BLUF openings:
- "[Primary keyword] requires [key factor]. Here's what that means for you."
- "The [best/right/correct] [topic] depends on [key variable]. Here's how to choose."
- "[Number] [types/options/methods] work for [use case]. [Top recommendation] is best for [most people] because [reason]."

---

## Closing patterns

Never end with:
- "In conclusion..."
- "We hope this article helped you..."
- "Don't hesitate to contact us..."

Good closings (100-150 words):
- Restate the core recommendation in 1 sentence
- Give one actionable next step
- Internal link CTA to a relevant collection or product: "Ready to find your match? Browse our [collection name]."

## Brand voice adaptation

If a store has a distinct brand voice, adapt these defaults:

**Signals of a more formal brand:** high-ticket products, B2B, medical/professional.
→ Reduce contractions, increase citation density, replace "you'll love" with "customers report".

**Signals of a playful brand:** apparel, gifts, lifestyle, youth market.
→ Use more second person, shorter sentences, rhetorical questions, light humor is OK.

**Signals of an expert brand:** supplements, sports, technical gear.
→ Lead with data, cite research, use precise technical terms (don't dumb down if the audience is expert).

When in doubt: Conversational Expert works for 80% of Shopify stores.
